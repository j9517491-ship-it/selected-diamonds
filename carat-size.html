Why two diamonds of the same weight look different

A diamond may carry more of its weight in its depth, which makes it face up smaller than its carat weight suggests. Another stone of identical weight may have a wider visible spread. Shape matters too: elongated ovals, pears and marquises often appear larger than a round diamond of equivalent weight, because more of the stone is visible from above.

This is why I compare millimetre measurements alongside carat weight. Two 1.00ct rounds can differ by a third of a millimetre across the table — small on paper, visible on a hand.

The figures used here are for well-cut stones. A poorly proportioned diamond of the same weight can measure noticeably smaller face up, which is one of the reasons cut quality matters more than the carat figure alone.
