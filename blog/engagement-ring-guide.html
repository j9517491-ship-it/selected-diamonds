Buying Guide

Buying an Engagement Ring, Step by Step

Buying an engagement ring is often the largest and most emotionally charged jewellery purchase someone will make. A clear process helps — here's the order I recommend to clients.

1. Set a realistic budget first

Decide your budget before you start looking at stones, not after. This single step prevents the most common source of buyer's remorse: falling for a diamond above what you meant to spend.

2. Learn the 4Cs before you shop

You don't need to become a gemologist, but understanding cut, colour, clarity and carat will help you evaluate options and avoid overpaying for grades that don't visibly matter. (See my 4Cs guide for the short version.)

3. Decide: natural or lab-grown

This is largely a question of budget and values rather than quality — both are real, certified diamonds. See my natural vs. lab-grown comparison if you're undecided.

4. Choose a shape that fits the wearer

Round brilliant remains the most popular for its brightness, but oval, cushion, emerald and pear cuts each suit different tastes and hand shapes. If you're buying as a surprise, subtle research into existing jewellery preferences goes a long way.

5. Insist on independent certification

Only consider diamonds with a report from a recognised independent laboratory. Certification is what makes the 4Cs meaningful — without it, you're taking someone's word for quality you can't verify.

6. Get the setting and sizing right

A well-chosen stone can be undermined by a setting that doesn't protect it in daily wear, or a ring size that's guessed rather than measured. Where possible, get a discreet, accurate ring size before ordering.

7. Buy from someone who explains, not just sells

The right advisor will walk you through trade-offs honestly, show you the certification, and never rush the decision. That's the whole premise behind Selected Diamonds.

Confidence in your choice is just as important as the diamond itself.
