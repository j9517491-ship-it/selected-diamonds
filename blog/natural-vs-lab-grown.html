Natural vs. Lab-Grown Diamonds: An Honest Comparison

Lab-grown diamonds are chemically, physically and optically identical to natural diamonds — they are real diamonds, not simulants like cubic zirconia or moissanite. The only meaningful difference is where they were formed: deep in the earth over billions of years, or in a controlled laboratory environment over a matter of weeks.

Cost

Lab-grown diamonds typically cost 60–85% less than a natural diamond of comparable size and quality. For clients prioritising a larger stone or a lower budget, this is often the deciding factor.

Rarity and value retention

Natural diamonds benefit from genuine geological scarcity, which has historically supported resale and heirloom value. Lab-grown diamonds can be produced at scale, and their retail prices have been falling — meaning they generally hold less resale value over time. If long-term value retention matters to you, this is worth weighing carefully.

Environmental claims and sourcing

Lab-grown diamonds are widely marketed as the sustainable choice. I am not persuaded by that claim, and I would rather say so than repeat it.

Growing a diamond means holding a chamber at extreme temperature and pressure for weeks. It is energy-intensive, and whether that energy is clean depends entirely on the grid the reactor is plugged into — a great deal of production takes place where electricity still comes largely from coal. The carbon figures quoted in advertising generally originate with producers themselves, and there is no independently audited standard behind them. In 2019 the US Federal Trade Commission wrote to eight lab-grown sellers warning that unqualified claims such as “eco-friendly” and “sustainable” were unlikely to be substantiated. FTC: warning letters on diamond ad disclosures

None of this makes mining harmless. Mining moves an enormous quantity of earth and consumes water and energy of its own. My point is narrower: neither origin is environmentally free, and I do not think the available evidence supports choosing lab-grown on environmental grounds alone.

Sourcing is where I can be concrete. I buy natural stones through reputable, traceable supply chains, and I will tell you what is known — and what is not — about a particular stone's history before you commit to it.

Certification either way

Whichever you choose, certification is non-negotiable. Reputable labs clearly identify lab-grown stones on their reports, and a trustworthy seller will never blur that distinction. I only work with certified stones of either origin, and I explain the trade-offs plainly before you decide.

There's no universally "right" choice — only the right choice for your budget, timeline and values.
